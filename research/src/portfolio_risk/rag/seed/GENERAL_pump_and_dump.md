# Pump-and-dump schemes (plain English)

This is educational information, not investment advice.

## What is a pump and dump?
A pump and dump is a scheme where people push a thinly traded stock higher with hype, then sell their own shares to the
buyers the hype attracted. The price rises fast on loud promotion, and collapses when the promoters sell. Late buyers
are left holding shares that lost most of their value.

## Common warning signs
Warning signs include a sudden surge in social media mentions of a small stock, many brand-new accounts posting the
same message, copied and pasted posts, a sharp price jump with unusual trading volume, and no real news or company
filing that explains the move. Penny stocks and very small companies are the usual targets because a modest amount of
money can move the price.

## Hype is not the same as manipulation
A stock can also jump because of real news such as an earnings report, a contract or an acquisition. A move that
follows a real catalyst is an ordinary move, so a good detector checks whether news or a filing came first. Even a
heavily discussed stock may be moving for honest reasons, and a high risk signal is a reason to look closer, not proof
of wrongdoing.

## What a short squeeze is
A short squeeze happens when investors who bet against a stock are forced to buy shares back as the price rises, which
pushes the price up further. Meme stocks such as GME and AMC showed this in 2021. A squeeze can look like a pump but
comes from the mechanics of short selling, not necessarily from a coordinated scheme.

## How to protect yourself
Be careful with any stock you first hear about through a flood of posts. Check for real news and filings, look at
trading volume, never buy only because a price is rising, and only risk money you can afford to lose. This
information does not predict future prices.
